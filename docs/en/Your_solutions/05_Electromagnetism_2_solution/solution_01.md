To calculate the electric flux through a spherical surface, we can use **Gauss’s Law**, which provides a direct relationship between the net electric flux through a closed surface and the enclosed charge.

### 1. Identify the Given Information
*   **Point Charge ($q$):** $+2\text{ C}$
*   **Radius of Sphere ($r$):** $1\text{ m}$ (centered at the origin)
*   **Permittivity of Free Space ($\varepsilon_0$):** approximately $8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ F/m}$ (or $\text{C}^2/\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2$)

---

### 2. State Gauss’s Law
Gauss’s Law states that the total electric flux ($\Phi_E$) through any closed surface is equal to the total enclosed charge ($Q_{\text{enc}}$) divided by the permittivity of free space:

$$\Phi_E = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

---

### 3. Step-by-Step Calculation

**Step A: Determine the Enclosed Charge**
Since the charge is located at the origin and the spherical surface is centered at the origin with a radius of $1\text{ m}$, the entire $+2\text{ C}$ charge is inside the sphere.
*   $Q_{\text{enc}} = 2\text{ C}$

**Step B: Substitute Values into the Formula**
$$\Phi_E = \frac{2}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}}$$

**Step C: Solve for Numerical Value**
$$\Phi_E \approx 2.258 \times 10^{11}\text{ V}\cdot\text{m}$$

---

### 4. Final Result
The total electric flux through the spherical surface is:

$$\mathbf{\Phi_E \approx 2.26 \times 10^{11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}}$$

> **Note on Radius:** In this specific problem, the radius of the sphere ($1\text{ m}$) does not affect the total flux. According to Gauss's Law, as long as the charge is fully enclosed, the flux depends only on the magnitude of the charge, not the size or shape of the container.
