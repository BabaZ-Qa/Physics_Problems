## 9. Refraction (Snell's Law)
**Problem:** A light ray travels from air ($n_1 = 1.00$) into glass ($n_2 = 1.50$). If the angle of incidence is $30^\circ$, what is the angle of refraction?

### Solution:
*   **Refractive Index of Air ($n_1$):** $1.00$
*   **Refractive Index of Glass ($n_2$):** $1.50$
*   **Angle of Incidence ($\theta_1$):** $30^\circ$

---

### Step 1: State Snell's Law
Snell's Law relates the indices of refraction and the angles of the light ray in two different media:
$$n_1 \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \sin(\theta_2)$$

---

### Step 2: Step-by-Step Calculation

**A. Substitute the known values:**
$$(1.00) \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = (1.50) \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$

**B. Calculate $\sin(30^\circ)$:**
We know that $\sin(30^\circ) = 0.5$.
$$1.00 \cdot 0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$
$$0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$

**C. Isolate $\sin(\theta_2)$:**
$$\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{0.5}{1.50}$$
$$\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333$$

**D. Solve for $\theta_2$ (Inverse Sine):**
$$\theta_2 = \arcsin(0.3333)$$

---

### Final Result:
*   **Angle of Refraction ($\theta_2$):** $\approx 19.47^\circ$

> **Physical Insight:** Since the light is moving from a medium with a lower refractive index (air) to a higher one (glass), the ray bends **toward the normal**, resulting in an angle of refraction smaller than the angle of incidence.
