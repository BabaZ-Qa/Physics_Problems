To find the current at a specific moment in time, we use the definition of instantaneous current, which is the rate of change of charge with respect to time.

---

### **Step 1: Understand the Relationship**

Current ($I$) is the derivative of the charge ($Q$) with respect to time ($t$):


$$I(t) = \frac{dQ}{dt}$$

### **Step 2: Differentiate the Function**

Given the function:


$$Q(t) = 5t^2 + 5$$

We apply the power rule for differentiation ($d/dt [at^n] = n \cdot at^{n-1}$):

* The derivative of $5t^2$ is $2 \cdot 5t = 10t$.
* The derivative of a constant (5) is $0$.

So, the formula for the current at any time $t$ is:


$$I(t) = 10t$$

### **Step 3: Calculate for $t = 3\,\text{s}$**

Now, substitute $3$ for $t$ into the derivative:


$$I(3) = 10(3)$$

$$I(3) = 30$$

---

### **Final Answer**

The current at $t = 3\,\text{s}$ is **$30\,\text{A}$**.
