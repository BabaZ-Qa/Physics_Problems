To find the average current, we use the relationship between total charge and the time interval over which it flows.

---

### **Step 1: Identify the Given Values**

First, we list the variables provided in the problem and convert them into standard SI units (Amperes, Coulombs, and Seconds):

* **Charge ($Q$):** $30\,\text{C}$
* **Time ($t$):** $2\,\text{ms}$

Since $1\,\text{millisecond} = 10^{-3}\,\text{seconds}$, we convert the time:


$$t = 2 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{s} = 0.002\,\text{s}$$

---

### **Step 2: Use the Average Current Formula**

The average current ($I_{avg}$) is defined as the total charge divided by the total time:


$$I_{avg} = \frac{Q}{\Delta t}$$

### **Step 3: Calculate the Result**

Substitute the values into the formula:


$$I_{avg} = \frac{30\,\text{C}}{0.002\,\text{s}}$$

To make the division easier, you can think of it as:


$$I_{avg} = \frac{30}{2} \times 10^3$$

$$I_{avg} = 15 \times 1000$$

$$I_{avg} = 15,000\,\text{A}$$

---

### **Final Answer**

The average current of the lightning bolt is **$15,000\,\text{A}$** (or **$15\,\text{kA}$**).
