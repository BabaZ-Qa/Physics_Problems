To find the number of turns in the secondary coil, we use the transformer ratio formula. This formula links the voltage across the coils directly to the number of turns they have.

### The Formula

$$\frac{V_p}{V_s} = \frac{N_p}{N_s}$$

Where:

* $V_p$ = primary voltage (**120 V**)
* $V_s$ = secondary voltage (**9.0 V**)
* $N_p$ = number of turns in the primary coil (**400 turns**)
* $N_s$ = number of turns in the secondary coil (what we need to find)

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Rearrange the formula to solve for $N_s$**
To isolate $N_s$, we can cross-multiply and rearrange the equation:


$$N_s = N_p \times \frac{V_s}{V_p}$$

**Step 2: Plug in the known values**


$$N_s = 400 \times \frac{9.0}{120}$$

**Step 3: Calculate the result**
First, simplify the fraction:


$$\frac{9.0}{120} = 0.075$$

Now, multiply by the primary turns:


$$N_s = 400 \times 0.075 = 30$$

### Final Answer

The secondary coil must have **30 turns**.
